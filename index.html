Abstract

A reward pipeline that stays readable

Pairwise visual preference labels are useful but opaque: a judge can choose the better image without ever making its standard explicit. Auto-Rubric as Reward addresses this gap by first eliciting concrete criteria from a vision-language model, then verifying and refining those criteria against supervision before reusing them as a structured reward specification. The result is a reward pipeline that is inspectable, reusable, and directly aligned with the actual generation task rather than a latent aesthetic prior.

Why explicit rubrics?

Better reward signals begin with visible criteria

Inspectable

Reward behavior is written in plain language. Researchers can read, edit, prune, and version-control the criteria instead of treating reward modeling as a black box.

Verifiable

Candidate rubrics are not accepted on faith. They must explain the labeled preference pair and are revised when the judge fails to recover the expected ordering.

Reusable

Once verified, rubric sets can be reused across runs, benchmark settings, and judge backends for deterministic and auditable experiments.

Method

From preference examples to ARR-RPO rewards

Step 1

Query-specific rubric generation

A labeled pair provides the preference signal; the VLM then proposes concrete dimensions such as prompt adherence, structural fidelity, edit accuracy, or artifact control.

Step 2

Verification and refinement

The same rubric must recover the ground-truth ranking. Failed criteria are revised rather than silently passed through.

Step 3

Structured reward reuse

Verified rubrics are grouped into reusable theme-tip structures, then consumed by a frozen VLM judge whose pairwise decisions are converted into ARR rewards.

Results

Consistent gains across evaluators, preference benchmarks, and generation benchmarks

We report two complementary views of performance: evaluator accuracy on human-labeled preference benchmarks, and downstream generative quality on text-to-image and image-editing benchmarks.

What the results suggest

  • Explicit criteria improve alignment without sacrificing interpretability.
  • Task-aware rubrics reduce the tendency to reward generic prettiness.
  • Image editing especially benefits from preservation-aware judging.
  • Reusable rubric files make long ARR-RPO runs easier to audit and reproduce.

Get started

Run the pipeline with your own judge

The repository includes the full Auto-Rubric pipeline, example datasets, reusable rubric files, and RPO training launchers for both FLUX text-to-image and Qwen-Image-Edit.

conda activate vision-auto-rubric
bash env_setup.sh

MODEL_PATH=Qwen/Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct TP_SIZE=1 PORT=8000 \
  bash rubric_pipeline/vllm_serve.sh

  --test_dataset examples/test_t2i_pairwise.json \
  --base_url http://localhost:8000/v1
